## Reading

1. **The factored channel transfers to never-seen IDs.** A frozen Qwen3-1.7B sender and a frozen
   SmolLM2-360M receiver, joined by sender-computed letter, digit and color codes and one trained
   reader, communicate color-to-ID bindings for 56 IDs that never appeared in any training or
   development table, including letter-digit pairs that break the training block structure. It
   comes within about 3.5 points of the text handoff (93.8% vs 97.3%) with near-zero controls, and
   changed-answer following shows the binding is read from the channel, not guessed.
2. **Ensembling matters.** Single members score 72-84%; the three-seed ensemble scores 93.8%.
   The DEV-to-FINAL drop was small for the ensemble (96.9% to 93.8%) and larger for members, as
   expected from selecting each member's step on DEV.
3. **The free-form latent does not transfer.** Continuous per-line sender slots fit trained IDs
   perfectly (FINAL-known 100%) but reach only 14.3% on never-seen IDs. In this setup, transfer
   to new identities requires the factored, symbolic structure.

## What this does and does not show

It shows that a learned, sender-computed, factored symbolic code, read by a trained reader inside
a frozen receiver, carries bindings of never-seen symbolic IDs across model families. The code is
close to a compressed text message: discrete letter, digit and color choices per table line.
It is not evidence for free-form latent communication, which the secondary arm tested directly and
which failed. No efficiency advantage over text, natural-language, Arabic or generation claim
follows.
